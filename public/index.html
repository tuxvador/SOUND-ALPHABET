How a word becomes one sound

Position-keyed banks  /  5 slots × 12 tones

Sounding a word's letters simultaneously is ambiguous if they all draw on the same two banks. Five letters would put five row tones and five column tones in the air at once, pairable twenty-five ways, and LISTEN and SILENT would be the same sound — same letters, same chord.

So each position in the word gets its own pair of banks. The first letter draws from one set of twelve frequencies, the second from a different twelve, and so on across five slots — sixty distinct tones spanning 400 to 3600 Hz. Because no two positions share a frequency, overlapping letters never compete: the pairing is unambiguous, order is preserved, and anagrams sound different. Every letter still rings for the full duration, so what you hear is one chord.

The tones are dealt round-robin rather than in blocks, so each slot's frequencies are spread across the whole band instead of clustered — no position lands entirely where a speaker or a room happens to be weak. Words longer than five letters break into consecutive chords, and the receiver reads each chord in a single shot once it has stopped ringing.

Receive

Goertzel detection  /  12 bins
Decoded text appears here.

Play the transmitter aloud through speakers with this listening — the message crosses the room as sound and reassembles itself. The receiver runs twelve Goertzel filters, one per frequency in the two banks. Each reports the energy at exactly its own frequency, which is far cheaper than a full spectrum when you already know the only twelve places a tone can hide. The strongest row and the strongest column must both clear the noise floor by a set margin before a character is accepted, so room noise alone never spells anything.
